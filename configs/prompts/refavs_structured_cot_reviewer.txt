Review rules:
- Split the reference into every condition needed to identify the instance:
  semantic head/category, appearance/action, negation, sounding or silent
  status, spatial relation and anchor, temporal order, continuity, duration,
  loudness, pitch, rhythm, and superlative/comparative constraints.
- For every audio-related template, split the reference into at least two
  atomic conditions: an acoustic condition supported directly by
  audio_evidence and a visual identity/action/spatial condition supported
  directly by visual_evidence.
- Every reference condition must have a corresponding condition_checks entry.
- A stage must provide direct, specific evidence, not merely restate the
  reference. "A drum set is visible" does not establish which drum is biggest;
  the evidence must compare the candidate drums or identify the visibly largest
  one. General knowledge that a bass drum is usually largest is insufficient.
- For every covered condition, evidence_quote must be copied verbatim from the
  named evidence stage. Do not quote reference_analysis or decision as evidence.
- cross_modal_reasoning may explain the binding only after its acoustic and
  visual premises have separate checks. It cannot be the sole evidence for a
  sounding, action, identity, appearance, anchor, or spatial condition.
- For a covered condition, evidence_stage must be exactly audio_evidence,
  visual_evidence, or cross_modal_reasoning. Never cite reference_analysis or
  decision. For an uncovered condition, use evidence_stage="none" and an empty
  evidence_quote.
- Evidence for one object cannot establish a property of another object. A
  guitar sound does not establish that an accordion is sounding.
- First/last, before/after, longest/shortest, louder/quieter, highest/lowest,
  continuous/intermittent, and biggest/smallest require an explicit comparison
  or observation at the requested level.
- When several same-category instances are visible, the evidence must uniquely
  identify which instance satisfies the comparison or relation. "One drum is
  visibly the largest" is insufficient because it does not identify that drum;
  the evidence must distinguish it from the other drums by a visible location,
  role, or appearance supported by the media.
- For an audio-related template, audio facts belong in audio_evidence, visual
  facts belong in visual_evidence, and cross_modal_reasoning must combine them.
  For a non-audio template, the draft must not rely on audio.
- Reject any audio_evidence that contains a visual spatial relation such as
  left/right/behind/near, a visual appearance such as long-haired, or an actor
  attribution such as "played by the man." Audio may establish a sound or
  acoustic property; visual_evidence must establish who performs an action and
  where objects are located.
- Reject anonymous acoustic evidence such as "a sound is produced by an
  object." For a cross-modal binding to a specific visible target, the audio
  stage must identify the audible source/category or a distinctive audible
  event that the visual stage can independently bind to. The conclusion cannot
  introduce that identity for the first time.
- The decision must follow from the evidence and preserve the canonical target.
- List any factual claim that is unsupported by both the supplied media and the
  source CoT in unsupported_claims. Unnecessary brands, object subtypes,
  clothing, colors, positions, actions, or acoustic properties are failures.
- Compute pass conservatively. pass may be true only when every condition is
  covered, all three boolean checks are true, and both unsupported_claims and
  issues are empty.
- Return JSON only, without Markdown or additional commentary.
